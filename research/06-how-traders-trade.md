## Section A: The Daily Routine of a Profitable Day Trader (Composite)

This is a composite of SMB Capital's documented desk routine, Lance Breitstein's interview disclosures (Trillium), Adam Mancini's published methodology, and the routine the Topstep blog publishes for its survivor cohort. Times are US Eastern.

### 4:30–6:30 AM — The Overnight Read

The serious intraday traders are NOT the 4 AM warriors of YouTube clickbait. The actual schedule for SMB-style equity traders and CME futures traders looks like:

- **4:30–5:30 AM** (futures traders, Adam Mancini cohort): Glance at Asian close + European open. Check ES/NQ overnight range, any 02:00–04:00 spike that broke a prior-day level, and whether overnight session held or broke value.
- **6:00–7:00 AM** (equities, SMB-style): First substantive screen time. Pull up Benzinga Pro / Trade Ideas / Finviz. Filter: stocks gapping ≥4% on volume, fresh news catalyst, no obvious dilution risk. Stockbee's documented +4% scan runs here.
- The work is **filter + tag**, not analyze. You're building a watchlist of 6–15 names, not picking trades.

### 7:00–8:30 AM — Catalyst Verification + Levels

- Read the 8-K / press release for each gapper. Was it a real EPS beat or a guidance update? Was it dilutive financing dressed as "strategic partnership"? Brian Lee's process is brutal here — most "gappers" are dilution traps that will fade hard once filings hit EDGAR.
- Mark levels: prior-day high/low, premarket high/low, key Fib/round numbers, daily 20EMA and VWAP anchor for prior session. Adam Mancini publishes his ES levels at this time every day on Substack and X — `@AdamMancini4`.
- Macro: ISM/CPI/NFP/FOMC days require a different posture (smaller size, tighter stops, no trades into the print).

### 8:30–9:15 AM — Plan, Don't Trade

- Pre-market data drops at 8:30 (CPI, PPI, NFP, retail sales, GDP). The window from 8:30–9:00 is for ABSORBING the print, not trading it. Spreads are wide; algos rip first.
- SMB documented practice: each trader writes a one-page game plan for their top 2–3 ideas with **explicit triggers and invalidations** (not "watch this name" — actual: "long XYZ over 47.85 with stop at 47.40, first target 48.60"). Lance Breitstein's Trillium routine was the same: "two strongest ideas per person, exact triggers and invalidations."

### 9:30–9:45 AM — The Bell

**Almost no profitable discretionary trader trades the first 15 minutes blindly.** The opening range is being established. Spreads are wide. Algos are placing/canceling huge orders to map liquidity.

The exceptions are:
- Pre-defined gap-and-go setups with a hard 9:30 trigger (a Stockbee +4% gapper that opens above premarket high on volume).
- Failed-breakdown reclaims of overnight low (Adam Mancini's bread and butter; he often takes the FIRST 5 minutes' breakdown failure as his AM trade).
- News spikes with a clear directional bias.

For most working traders, 9:30–9:45 is "**watching, not trading**." Linda Raschke calls these 15 minutes "the bid-ask game" and explicitly waits for the opening range to print before acting.

### 9:45–11:00 AM — Prime Time

This is where the bulk of edge actually lives. The opening range has printed. Algos have shown their hand. Liquidity is at its deepest of the day.

- **Opening Range Breakouts/Failures** activate (Raschke, Breitstein, Mancini all trade this window).
- **VWAP rejections / VWAP reclaims** are most reliable here (SMB blog: VWAP works ~70–75% during 9:45–11:30).
- **Episodic Pivot continuations** — Stockbee's documented process flow has Day-1 EP entries firing in this window after the gap-fill attempt fails and the trend resumes.
- Risk management constraint: most prop-firm desks publish a "**three strikes and you're sized down**" rule for this window. Lance Breitstein: after two consecutive losses, take a 15-minute walk and cut size 50% for the rest of the session.

### 11:00 AM–1:00 PM — Lunch Chop

Volume drops 30–60%. Spreads widen. Breakouts fail more often than they continue. Almost every serious trader interviewed by Schwager, Bellafiore, and Chat With Traders flags this period as **the danger zone for over-trading**.

The discipline:
- Reduce size by 50–66% or stop trading.
- Use this time for **journaling the morning's trades** (most SMB traders do this between 11:30 and 12:30).
- **Sim / review / playbook entry**. Mike Bellafiore's documented desk practice is: every trader writes a one-page "best trade of the morning" Playbook entry by 1 PM.

### 1:00–3:00 PM — Reset

- Lower-conviction window. Profitable traders take fewer trades, often only on A+ setups.
- This is where most blowups happen. Topstep's published combine-failure data shows the highest concentration of stop-outs between 1:30 and 2:45 PM — traders trying to "make back" morning losses on stale setups.

### 3:00–4:00 PM — Power Hour

Volume returns. Index closes, MOC imbalance prints at 3:50. Decision time.

- **Profitable traders are LESS aggressive in power hour, not more.** Lance Breitstein's interview disclosure: "If I'm green going into 3 PM, I size down and protect the day. If I'm red, I size down even more — I'm done hunting."
- Mancini's published methodology: end-of-day failed-breakdowns at the prior-day-low can be A+ setups but ONLY if the rest of the day's structure supports it.
- **Closing imbalance trades** (3:50–4:00 PM) are a specialist niche — most desks don't trade them, but SMB has documented a handful of traders who only trade MOC.

### 4:00–6:00 PM — The Real Job

This is the work that compounds. After the close:
- **Journal entry** (Edgewonk, TradeZella, TraderSync, or a structured spreadsheet). The minimum viable entry: setup name, entry/stop/target, what triggered entry, what triggered exit, **process grade A–F**, one sentence on emotion.
- Review the day's trades on chart replay. Was the setup actually present? Was the entry timing on the actual signal or chased?
- Build tomorrow's watchlist seed (continuation names, earnings tomorrow, scheduled catalysts).
- Steenbarger's published advice — don't journal only outcomes, journal **process variables**: did you follow your rules, did you size correctly, did you exit on signal or emotion.

### Evening / Weekend — The Edge-Builder

- Saturday/Sunday: run the journal aggregator. Group trades by setup. Calculate win rate, expectancy, R-multiple distribution. This is how you find your **personal edge AND your personal leaks**. Most retail traders never do this and that is exactly why they stay retail.

## Section B: The Setup Playbook — What Real Working Traders Actually Trade

### B1. Failed Breakout / Failure Test (Al Brooks, Adam Mancini)

**Premise:** Brooks documents that >80% of trading-range breakouts fail. The breakout traps stops above resistance, then reverses. Mancini calls this his single highest-edge setup and says it's >70% of his entries.

**Setup:** Price breaks a clearly defined level (prior-day high, range top, key round number) on what looks like a breakout. Within 1–5 bars, price reclaims the level and closes back inside the range.

**Entry:** On the close of the reclaim bar, OR on the break of the reclaim bar's high (for shorts) / low (for longs going the other way).

**Stop:** Above the failed-breakout extreme (the wick that trapped the longs). Mancini sizes stops at "max 1R = predefined dollar amount" regardless of where the chart says.

**Target:** Opposite end of the range. Often a measured-move equal to the range height.

**Why it works:** Trapped breakout buyers are forced sellers. Their stops feed the reversal. Big money does this on purpose at obvious levels.

### B2. Opening Range Break / ORB Failure (Linda Raschke, Lance Breitstein)

**Premise:** First 15 or 30 min establishes initial equilibrium. Decisive break of that range with volume tends to extend.

**Setup:** Mark 9:30–9:45 high and low. Wait. ORB long = break of high on volume with no immediate rejection. ORB failure = breaks high, immediately reclaims below = potential short back to opposing side of range.

**Entry:** On the break of OR-high (long) with confirmation candle (Raschke style), OR on the reclaim bar of OR-high (failure-test style).

**Stop:** Opposing side of opening range (usually 0.4–0.6 ATR), or tighter at the structural pivot.

**Target:** 1R initial, 2–3R extension if trend day signals (Raschke "trend day" identifiers — open near low, narrow Wpr, hold above OR-high all day).

### B3. VWAP Rejection / VWAP Reclaim (SMB Capital, Adam Mancini)

**Premise:** Institutions use VWAP as execution benchmark. They want to buy below and sell above. This creates real S/R at VWAP ~70–75% of active hours.

**Setup (rejection short):** Stock opens weak, sells off, establishes below VWAP. Mid-morning rally back UP to VWAP from below on declining volume. VWAP holds as ceiling.

**Entry:** Red confirmation candle at VWAP. Enter on close of red candle OR break of its low.

**Stop:** Above VWAP + small buffer (often 0.25–0.5 ATR).

**Target:** Day's low first; trail with intraday structure.

**Variant (reclaim long):** Stock rejects from below VWAP early, then reclaims VWAP with volume. Reclaim becomes new support. Long the reclaim bar with stop below VWAP.

### B4. Episodic Pivot / Earnings Gap-and-Go (Stockbee / Pradeep Bonde)

**Premise:** True Episodic Pivots are sudden game-changing events (earnings beat + guidance raise, FDA approval, M&A) that force institutions to re-rate the stock. They're not one-day moves — they often run 5–20 days.

**Setup (Stockbee's published scan):**
- Close vs prior close: `c/c1 > 1.04` (4% gap up)
- Volume: `v > 3 * avgv50` (3x average volume)
- Volume floor: `v >= 300,000`
- Catalyst: real news, not just a sympathy move

**Entry:** Day 1 enter on opening drive if the theme is strong and gap holds. Day 2–3 enter on the first pullback that holds above breakout level (this is the higher-probability variant per Bonde's interviews).

**Stop:** Below the consolidation low / breakout pivot. NEVER below the gap — that's too wide.

**Target:** Bonde describes 8–20% moves over 3–5 days as the typical Momentum Burst window. Trail with 10EMA or 8EMA.

**Hard rule:** "Never buy after 3+ consecutive up days." That's a Bonde cardinal rule because the move has already happened.

### B5. Holy Grail Mean Reversion (Linda Raschke)

**Premise:** When ADX(14) >30 (strong trend) and price retraces to the 20-period EMA, odds favor a retest of the most recent high (long) / low (short).

**Setup:** Daily or intraday — ADX(14) > 30 confirms trend exists. Wait for pullback to 20EMA. Stop at 20EMA, not before.

**Entry:** Buy the touch of 20EMA in an uptrend (or short the touch in a downtrend), with confirmation = reversal bar at the EMA.

**Stop:** Below the most recent swing low (long version). Tight, structural.

**Target:** Most recent swing high. If broken, trail.

**Note:** Raschke publicly acknowledged in recent webinars that many *Street Smarts* setups have decayed via widespread adoption. She specifically said Holy Grail still works. The Turtle Soup and 80/20 setups have weaker edges now than in the 90s.

### B6. News-Catalyst Momentum (Cleaner Variant of Ross Cameron)

**Premise:** News on small/mid-cap that creates immediate institutional buying. Different from the Warrior-Trading-style 24/7 chasing of every micro-cap pump.

**Setup:** Stock gaps on real news (earnings, FDA, contract). Float < 50M. Volume > 5x average. Premarket high holds on first pullback.

**Entry:** Break of premarket high in first 30 minutes, OR first pullback to VWAP that holds.

**Stop:** VWAP or last consolidation low.

**Target:** Round number + measured move from premarket range.

**Honest caveat:** This is what Ross Cameron *teaches*. The FTC sued Warrior Trading in 2022 and they settled for $3M for misleading marketing. The setup itself can work; the typical-student-outcome marketing was the problem. Brian Lee shorts the BACK side of these — same setup, opposite direction, when the move is overextended.

### B7. Marci's Little RZY Measured Move (User's Existing Reference)

Per `01-marci-silfrain-deep-dive.md` in this research directory — measured-move math on a clean structural pivot. The discipline is mechanical entry, hard stop, target at the 1:1 measured projection. Marci's "plan to lose 20 in a row" sizing math (Section D below) makes this an industrial process, not a punt.

### B8. Order-Flow Absorption (Trader Dale, FT71)

**Premise:** At a key S/R level, you see unusually large volume on BOTH bid AND ask in the footprint chart — buyers absorbing sellers (or vice versa). The level is being defended by size.

**Setup:** Identify level in advance (volume profile POC, prior-day high, value-area edge). Watch footprint as price approaches.

**Entry trigger:** Heavy two-sided volume prints at the level AND price stalls (delta starts flipping). Enter on the first reversal bar in the direction of absorption.

**Stop:** Beyond the level (not arbitrary points — structural).

**Target:** Next volume node / structural level.

**Reality check:** Order flow is a real edge in futures (ES, NQ, CL) where the order book is deep enough to read. It is much weaker in equities (especially small caps with hidden / iceberg / off-exchange flow). Most retail "footprint" courses oversell.

## Section C: Prop Firm Reality

### C1. The Rules (2026, verified)

**Topstep Trading Combine:**
- Daily Loss Limit: $1,000 / $2,000 / $3,000 on $50K / $100K / $150K accounts. **Note:** As of Aug 2024 the default DLL was removed on TopstepX itself; Personal DLL is opt-in. NinjaTrader/Tradovate/Quantower/TradingView still enforce DLL on Combine.
- Max Loss Limit: trailing intraday; locks at starting balance once profits push the floor there.
- Profit target: 6% of account.
- Min trading days: 2 days, $200 minimum profit per day to count.
- Reset at 5 PM CT (CME open).
- **Documented pass rate (2025 cohort data on topstep.com):** 16.8% of Combines pass. 51.8% of participants eventually advance — meaning many restart 2–3+ times.

**Apex Trader Funding (post-4.0 March 2026):**
- Two drawdown types: End-of-Day Trailing OR Intraday Trailing.
- Safety Net = initial balance + drawdown + $100. Once you hit Safety Net, trailing stops.
- Rithmic/Tradovate platform-enforced stop-loss + take-profit on every order (March 2026 change).
- 5:1 R:R restriction is GONE. 30% consistency rule loosened to 50%.
- No daily loss limit on evaluations (but the trailing drawdown effectively functions as one).

**FundedNext:**
- CFDs: no consistency rule.
- Futures: 40% guideline — single day's profit ideally not >40% of total.
- Two-phase evaluation: Phase 1 = 10% profit target, Phase 2 = 5%, min 5 days each.

**The5%ers High-Stakes:**
- Phase 1: 8% target. Phase 2: 5% target.
- 5% daily loss, 10% max loss.
- Min 0.5% profit over 3 days (kills one-shot lucky passes).
- Scaling to $4M, profit splits 50% → 100%.

### C2. Why Most Combine Traders Fail

Aggregating Topstep, FundedNext, Apex, and ThinkCapital published failure analyses:

**Top reasons (in order of frequency):**

1. **Revenge trading after first loss** — most common single failure mode. Lose first trade, immediately enter second with larger size, lose 3x what the first cost.
2. **Oversizing on Day 1** — trader enters with max contracts to "show conviction," takes one wide move against, blows drawdown in hours.
3. **Time-pressure overtrading** — deadline approaches, profit target not hit, criteria get lowered. Trader who normally takes 3–4 trades per WEEK starts taking 2–3 per DAY.
4. **No written plan** — entering on screen feeling rather than predefined setups with explicit triggers.
5. **Trading the chop window** (1–2:30 PM ET on equities, lunch lull on futures).

**Industry pass rate:** 10–20% on first attempt across major firms. Only ~7% of all challenge buyers ever receive a payout.

### C3. What the Survivors Do Differently

From the survivor interviews (Topstep blog featuring funded traders, Lance Breitstein interview on funded-trader path, Apex testimonials):

- **Trade well below max size for the first 5–10 days.** Take 1/3 to 1/2 the contracts the platform allows. Survivors build cushion first, then increase size.
- **One setup, well-defined.** Survivors almost universally describe trading ONE setup repeatedly, not 5. They become expert in one structure.
- **Hard daily stop.** Self-imposed loss cap well INSIDE the platform DLL (e.g., $500 personal stop on a $1,000 DLL). Hit it = done for the day.
- **No trading the first hour after a losing session opens.** Sit out, watch, only enter once they see their setup print clearly.
- **No trading during macro prints** (CPI, NFP, FOMC). Survivors flat or tiny size.

### C4. Realistic Income Expectations

- Pass rate: 5–10% on first try; cumulative across retries roughly 15–25%.
- Payout: 80–90% to trader at most futures firms after passing.
- Median **payout amount for funded traders that get paid at all** is small — typically $500–$3,000 per payout cycle. The "$10K/month from $50K combine" narrative is a marketing artifact; survivor-bias case study.
- The realistic prop-firm math: combine fee $150–$300 attempts × 3–5 attempts average = $500–$1,500 sunk cost to get funded, then a multi-month grind to scale.

## Section D: Sizing Reality

### D1. Why Sizing Kills More Traders Than Setup Selection

The Schwager Market Wizards books, the Topstep failure analyses, and Steenbarger's clinical work all converge on the same finding: traders who learn good setups still blow up. Traders who learn good *sizing discipline* almost always survive, even with mediocre setups.

Quoted from `Market Wizards`: "There are old traders and bold traders, but very few old and bold traders."

### D2. The 1% / 2% Rules

- **1% rule:** Risk no more than 1% of account equity on any single trade (the "risk" = entry-to-stop distance × position size).
- **2% rule:** Same logic, higher tolerance. Most retail-trading books cite this.
- **Origin:** No single inventor; popularized in van Tharp's *Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom* in the 1990s. Some attribute earlier mention to Edwin Lefèvre's *Reminiscences*.
- **Reality:** Most retail accounts are too small for 1% to be meaningful. On a $5,000 account, 1% = $50 risk = tiny position. Traders override it. That override is usually what kills them.

### D3. Marci's "Plan to Lose 20 in a Row" Math (User's Existing Rule)

Per `01-marci-silfrain-deep-dive.md` — size each bet such that 20 consecutive losses still leaves you with enough capital to keep trading. If you risk 1% per trade, 20 consecutive losses = ~18% drawdown. Survivable. If you risk 5%, 20 in a row = 64% drawdown. Probably account-ending. This is just the mathematical floor of "don't sequence yourself into ruin."

### D4. Kelly Criterion in Practice

**Full Kelly formula** (for binary win/loss):

```
f* = (bp - q) / b
```

where `p` = win prob, `q` = loss prob, `b` = win/loss ratio.

- **Full Kelly maximizes long-term geometric growth** — but at the cost of brutal drawdowns. A 50% drawdown is mathematically expected.
- **Half Kelly** ≈ 75% of full-Kelly growth rate with about **half the max drawdown**. Most quants treat this as the practical default.
- **Quarter Kelly** is conservative — ~ half the growth, but very small max-DD risk.
- **The Rotando-Thorp 1992 finding:** Half-Kelly users had 90% probability of avoiding ruin over long periods, vs 50% for Full-Kelly users when there is estimation error in `p` and `b`.
- **Required:** At least 50–100 closed trades before Kelly inputs are statistically meaningful. With <50 trades, Kelly inputs are noisy and the formula will mis-size you, usually too large.

### D5. The Psychological vs Mathematical Optimum

They diverge. The mathematical optimum (Half Kelly) often produces drawdowns that drive traders out of the seat — they reduce size or quit at the worst moment. Steenbarger's clinical observation: most traders' *behaviorally sustainable* size is 30–50% of their *mathematically optimal* size. Trade where you can hold the line through a 6-trade losing streak without changing behavior. That's your real Kelly.

## Section E: The Journal

### E1. What Best Traders Journal (Steenbarger, SMB, Marci)

**Process variables, not just P&L.** Steenbarger's documented framework: every trade entry captures (1) plan beforehand, (2) emotion during, (3) outcome after, (4) what you'd do differently. P&L is the by-product of process; logging only P&L hides the actual edge or leak.

SMB's Playbook template: every "best trade" entry has — Big Picture, Intraday Fundamentals, Technicals, Tape Reading, Intuition. Plus a screenshot, entry rule, exit rule, and lesson-to-keep.

### E2. Tools

- **TradeZella** (~$30/mo) — best UI in 2026, always-on AI, 500+ broker imports, trade replay, playbooks, backtesting. Most-recommended for serious retail.
- **TraderSync** (~$80/mo Elite) — 700+ broker integrations, AI gated to Elite tier. Best for traders using many brokers/instruments.
- **Edgewonk** (~$197/yr) — strongest psychology/behavioral analytics, the Edge Finder algorithm. Narrower broker support. Cheapest serious option.
- **Tradervue** — older but established; popular at SMB.
- **DIY spreadsheet** — works for under 5 trades/day. Marci documents manual collection as a forcing function for awareness.

### E3. Minimum Viable Journal Entry

If you take five trades per day, this is the floor:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Time | 10:14 |
| Symbol | NQ |
| Setup name | Failed-breakdown reclaim 19,840 |
| Entry / Stop / T1 | 19,847 / 19,832 / 19,895 |
| Why I entered | Reclaim bar closed with volume after a 5-min flush |
| Why I exited | Hit T1 mechanically; trailed remainder, stopped at 19,860 |
| Process grade A–F | B (chased the entry by 4 ticks) |
| Emotion | Calm at entry, slight fomo on chase |
| Lesson | Wait one bar on reclaim, not the first tick |

Five fields are not enough. Eight is. More than 12 and you'll skip the journal.

### E4. Using Journal Data to Find Edge AND Leak

Quarterly (minimum): aggregate trades by setup. For each setup compute:

- Number of trades
- Win rate
- Average win in R
- Average loss in R
- Expectancy = (winrate × avg win) − (lossrate × avg loss)
- Time-of-day distribution of trades + P&L

The patterns you find:
- **Edge** is usually one or two setups that produce 60–80% of profits. Bellafiore calls this the trader's "bread and butter."
- **Leak** is usually one specific behavior — chasing extended entries, lunch trading, revenge after a loss, oversizing on second trade of the day.

The journal makes both visible. The trader without a journal is guessing.

## Section F: The Psychology Layer

### F1. Tilt Detection

Borrowed from poker. Tilt = emotion overriding logic. Steenbarger: when the amygdala hijacks the prefrontal cortex, you stop pattern-matching and start reacting.

**Early warning signs documented across multiple sources:**
- Rapid breathing or clenched jaw
- Tunnel vision on one ticker
- Internal monologue starting with "I need to" or "I have to"
- Thoughts about money owed / made rather than process
- Sizing up the next trade after a loss
- Mental fog after 3+ hours of screen time

### F2. The Step-Away Rule

Almost universal among working traders (Marci, Schwager interviewees, Topstep survivor cohort, Bellafiore):

- **2 losses in a row → 15-min walk away from the screen.**
- **3 losses → done for the day.** No exceptions.
- **Hit daily stop → flat, all positions closed, charts off.**

Why it works: physiologically, the cortisol/adrenaline cycle from a loss has a half-life of about 20–30 minutes. Trading while those hormones are peaked is trading while drunk. The walk-away forces the chemistry to reset.

### F3. Process Journaling vs Outcome Journaling (Steenbarger)

Outcome journaling: "Lost $400 today on three trades." Information-poor.

Process journaling: "Trade 1 was a planned setup, executed cleanly, lost on a wider-than-usual stop-out — that's variance. Trade 2 was a chased entry after Trade 1's loss, no setup, deserved to lose. Trade 3 was revenge sizing." Information-rich. Tells you exactly what to fix.

Steenbarger: "Effective journaling bridges the gap between tracking outcomes and understanding the psychological and behavioral patterns that drive trading decisions."

### F4. Why Discretionary Traders Fail Despite Good Setups

Bellafiore's documented breakdown of SMB combine washouts: 80% of failures are behavioral, not analytical. Specifically:

- Inability to take the loss when stop hits. Trader moves the stop, then takes a 3R loss instead of 1R.
- Inability to *stay in* the winner. Trader closes at +0.7R because "it might pull back."
- Inability to skip a no-setup day. Trader manufactures a setup because they "have to trade."

The setups are not the bottleneck. The seat behavior is.

## Section G: Systematic vs Discretionary

### G1. Andrea Unger — Pure Systematic

Unger won the World Cup Trading Championship 4 times (2008, 2009, 2010, 2012) with real money — the only trader to do so. His method:

- Run 30–80 strategies a month across markets and timeframes.
- Each strategy is fully coded with explicit entry, exit, and money management rules.
- Monte Carlo simulations decide which strategies stay in the live portfolio.
- "Discretion is the enemy of consistency." Unger explicitly says he trades only what the system tells him.

**Why it works for him:** Removes emotion. Diversifies across uncorrelated edges. Robust to single-strategy decay because the portfolio rotates.

**Why it fails for most retail:** Building a 30-strategy portfolio requires real coding skill, infrastructure, broker connectivity, and emotional discipline to NOT override a system in drawdown. Most retail "systematic" traders override at the worst moment.

### G2. Stockbee — Rules-Based with Discretion at Entry

Bonde's documented stance: the SETUP must be rule-based and scannable. The c/c1>1.04, v>3*avg, 9M EP — these are filters, not opinions. But entry timing within the setup is discretionary based on tape and macro context.

This is the **hybrid that works for active discretionary traders**: rules-based screen, discretionary execution within tight guardrails, mechanical exit.

### G3. The Hybrid Approach

Per documented commentary across Confluence, Topstep, ClearEdge:
- Systematic rules for **trade execution and risk management** (size, stop, daily loss cap).
- Discretion for **strategy selection** (which setup to take today) and **override decisions** (sit out FOMC, sit out a known earnings landmine).

Key warning from the systematic-vs-discretionary literature: **traders who run automation without frequent overrides outperform traders who run automation with active override habit, even when the overriders feel confident.** Override is psychologically rewarding but generally net-negative on P&L.

### G4. How the User Should Think About This (Bot + Discretion)

You already run multiple bots (AlphaTrader, AlphaOddsJam, BTCBash). The hybrid that makes sense for someone in your seat:

- **Bot does:** identification, sizing, execution, fill verification, exit on rule.
- **You do:** kill-switch decisions (macro regime change, system clearly broken, unusual market structure), capital allocation between bots, post-mortem analysis that feeds parameter updates.

The kill-switch should be a **pre-defined trigger list**, not a feeling. "Bot down 3% intraday across both detectors" is a trigger. "I have a bad feeling" is not — and the systematic-vs-discretionary literature is brutal about how badly "feelings" perform when bots have a measurable edge.

The discretion is at the **meta level** (which game to play, when to stop), not at the execution level (where the bot has structural advantages: no fatigue, no tilt, no slippage from hesitation).

## Honest Final Notes for the Reader

1. **Almost everyone who teaches trading sells something.** That's not automatically bad — Bellafiore, Raschke, Bonde all run their actual desks/strategies and also sell courses. The flag is when *all* a person produces is content, with no verifiable trading.

2. **Public P&L claims should be discounted by default.** Anyone showing a screenshot with no broker statement, no Verify badge, no third-party audit, is asking you to take their word. Lance Breitstein, Bellafiore, Unger — their track records are corroborated by third parties (firms, championships, audited returns). Most YouTube traders' are not.

3. **The single most repeated insight across every credible source above:** *sizing kills more accounts than setups.* This is the rule the user's existing memory files already encode (the 1% / 2% / Marci's 20-in-a-row rule). It is the rule that matters most.

4. **The bot-plus-discretion hybrid the user is already running** is closer to Unger's systematic approach than to discretionary day trading. The right reference frame is "small CTA shop with multiple uncorrelated strategies + human kill-switch," not "day trader at a prop desk." Calibrate the rest of this research accordingly.
